The highest-return direction

Fresh Frame has a strong native foundation and a useful coaching workflow. Its biggest opportunity is to make existing capabilities feel connected: one understandable timeline, a video-first editor, and unmistakable narration states. The interface currently asks users to move among several different control surfaces to finish one task.

PerspectiveAssessment
Everyday userImport is recognizable and the app stays focused on local video. The largest discovery gaps are finding saved work, selecting/editing an existing drawing, understanding pauses, and knowing how a finished MP4 differs from an editable backup.
Experienced reviewerThe tools are capable, but repeat work costs too many navigation and adjustment steps. Precise scrubbing, visible take playback, reusable styles, meaningful loops, and responsive mix edits offer the strongest efficiency gains.
iOS designerNative components are already in place. The priority is hierarchy, state clarity, direct manipulation, adaptive layout, and fewer disconnected panels. The recording row provides a concrete example of a layout that needs redesign rather than smaller text.
Recommended product direction: keep one persistent video canvas and time controller. Reveal drawing, narration, and export controls in context. Let a first-time user finish a simple review with very few decisions, while precise controls remain available when needed.

The recording, source, and existing test/plan documents support the findings below. Expected benefits are design judgments, not measured usability-study results. No app code, branch, pull request, release, or App Store setting was changed.

Architecture that matters for UX

LayerCurrent implementation
App entry and navigationSceneDelegate creates BJJNativeLibrary and presents BJJNativeHome through a SwiftUI hosting controller. Native review/editor screens are the primary iPhone experience.
Picture and interaction layerSwiftUI controls surround a UIKit canvas using AVPlayerLayer, Core Image-rendered annotations, and gesture recognizers. Geometry is normalized to the actual video picture.
Editing and persistenceBJJNativeEditorSession owns the document and transient editor state. The local store validates and atomically saves JSON plus retained assets. Inspector drafts and completed canvas gestures have different, explicit commit boundaries.
Media preparationNative Photos/Files import creates an owned media copy and a derived editing preview. Job services track cancellation, stages, failures, and bounded background execution. The current build configuration requests compatible Photos representation.
Narration and timeAVAudioRecorder creates retained takes. BJJReviewTimeline maps original-video time to finished-review time by inserting held frames; commentary can continue while the picture is frozen.
Preview and exportAVFoundation compositions map video, holds, and audio. Preview preparation builds a mixed PCM asset; export snapshots the saved revision and produces an ordinary H.264/AAC MP4 with annotations.
Recovery and portabilityBackup packages, protected copies of earlier projects, recording recovery, immutable export inputs, checkpoints, and Recently Deleted are implemented to varying degrees in the services; not all are exposed by the native UI.
Legacy implementationThe repository also contains React/TypeScript and FastAPI/FFmpeg paths, plus a Capacitor bridge. Those are not the current primary native screen hierarchy. Desktop timeline features were not credited as existing iPhone UI.

Good foundations to preserve

  • Preserve selected-item Photos access, local processing, retained originals, and the absence of an account requirement for editing.
  • Keep the distinction between a finished MP4 and an editable review, while making it clearer to users.
  • Preserve the existing timed-drawing geometry, atomic save transactions, and recoverable media ownership rules.
  • Keep pause-aware narration: it is valuable functionality and a strong basis for a distinctive explanation workflow.
  • Keep native sheets, system pickers, share sheets, semantic controls, and the optional tip model.
  • Treat the supplied recording as visual evidence of real usage, while using the pinned implementation to resolve feature details.

The review does not relist paused-drawing visibility, basic pause-and-talk narration, inspector collapse, inspector Delete, automatic editor opening after import, or basic backup/trash support as missing features. They are present in the inspected implementation. Older planning documents and screenshots are not treated as the current UI.

A simpler experience to aim for

Home

Select a video, continue the latest review, or open the full library. Preserve the branding and clear first-use state.

Review and draw

Keep the video and timeline stable. Selecting a drawing reveals its timing and style controls beside the picture.

Narrate

Use a focused dock with a timer, clear microphone state, Pause/Resume video, and Stop. Review takes with the video still visible.

Share and keep

Choose full review or a visible range, preview the result, save/share it, and retain access to completed exports and editable backups.

This is a proposed interaction direction, not a new tab hierarchy or implementation specification. New modes, panels, and features should earn their place through the tasks they simplify.

#1

Timeline & playback

Give the editor one zoomable, precise timeline

Priority 1 of 32Difficult effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed; current layout also visible in the recording

Current experience
The portrait editor has eight evenly spaced thumbnail buttons, a separate position slider, a horizontally scrolling strip of drawing cards, and a separate Cues sheet. Narration is managed elsewhere. The native main transport has no frame-step buttons, time-entry control, or zoomable time scale; the filmstrip and drawing strip disappear in landscape.
Proposed experience
Replace the disconnected time controls with one timeline beneath the video: thumbnails, a fixed playhead, and compact drawing/narration/paused-frame lanes. Start with a simple overview; expand only the selected lane. Let users drag to scrub, zoom the time scale with pinch or explicit +/− buttons, jump to the previous/next drawing or take, and tap the time to enter a position. Add fine-step buttons that accurately identify preview/output-frame stepping; true source-frame stepping needs a separate media implementation.
User benefit
Finding and revisiting the exact moment is central to every review. This makes long clips and dense annotations manageable and reduces repeated scrubbing.
Priority rationale
Highest overall value because it improves reviewing, drawing, narration alignment, looping, and range export. Deliver in stages rather than building a full multitrack editor at once.
How to evaluate it
Compare time and missed attempts when finding a specified grip change, revisiting a drawing, and aligning a short annotation in short and long clips.

#2

Narration & audio

Create a focused, unmistakable recording interface

Priority 2 of 32Moderate effortQuick-start package

Code confirmed and visually observed

Current experience
Recording puts a long state label, an unlabeled progress-style audio meter, Pause/Resume video, and Stop into one horizontal row. Disabled playback, mode, and history controls remain visible. In the recording at 56 seconds, the state label wraps across several short lines. There is no elapsed recording timer in this UI, although the recording object exposes elapsed time.
Proposed experience
When capture starts, use a dedicated compact recording dock: a single-line Recording status and elapsed timer; a clearly labeled microphone meter; then two large actions, Pause video/Resume video and Stop recording. While paused, say 'Video frozen · microphone recording.' Hide irrelevant disabled tools. Display the active microphone before capture, offer a short optional countdown, and show the headphone guidance on first use or relevant route changes rather than forcing the same confirmation every take.
User benefit
Users can tell whether their voice is still being captured and stop or resume confidently. It fixes an observed polish problem in a core feature.
Priority rationale
Very high benefit with a smaller scope than timeline work; the layout/state-label portion is a strong first delivery.
How to evaluate it
Verify readable controls in portrait, landscape, and accessibility text sizes; confirm users distinguish pausing video from stopping the microphone.

#3

Drawing & editing

Keep the video and playback available while editing a drawing

Priority 3 of 32Moderate effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed and visually corroborated

Current experience
Opening a drawing card or Cues entry switches to a properties layout with a scrollable Form and Save/Cancel. The normal filmstrip, transport, and tools are removed. The video remains visible, but the form contains its own seek slider and no play control. The existing collapsible inspector and staged draft are useful foundations.
Proposed experience
Use a contextual bottom inspector in portrait and a compact side inspector in landscape, with the same video and transport always available. Its first view should contain Timing, Style, Duplicate, and Delete; put numeric geometry and layer ordering behind More. Provide 'Preview this drawing' to play a small surrounding interval using the staged draft. Keep Apply/Cancel reachable even when the panel is collapsed. Preserve the existing atomic draft transaction.
User benefit
People can see the effect of an edit immediately and judge appearance and timing without repeatedly leaving and reopening properties.
Priority rationale
A frequent editing bottleneck with substantial existing infrastructure, so it ranks above most new features.
How to evaluate it
Have a user change a drawing's timing, replay it, resize it, and cancel the experiment without losing the saved version.

#4

Narration & audio

Make take playback visible beside the video

Priority 4 of 32Moderate effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed; sheet occlusion is a design inference pending device walkthrough

Current experience
Narration opens a modal Form; selecting a take pushes another Form. 'Preview in video' starts the underlying player, but the take screen provides no video view. Playback is not explicitly bounded to the take's end. Takes are named only Take 1, Take 2, and so on.
Proposed experience
Present takes in a compact panel alongside the visible video. Selecting a take highlights its span and exposes Play take, Mute, volume, and timing. Play from just before the take to just after it, then stop or loop. Offer optional short take names, such as 'Grip explanation,' while retaining automatic numbering as the default.
User benefit
Users can check that words match the action and identify the right take without navigating away from the footage.
Priority rationale
High confidence usability gain built on existing playback and take metadata; waveform editing can come later.
How to evaluate it
Measure whether users can identify and correct a misaligned take without closing the narration panel.

#5

Import & responsiveness

Make repeated audio and undo adjustments feel immediate

Priority 5 of 32Difficult effortLarger improvement

Preparation path is code confirmed; actual delay is unmeasured

Current experience
Each saved gain, mute, or take change can call prepareAudio. That routine builds a full-duration PCM preview and disables key interactions while preparing. Undo/redo also calls prepareAudio even when only a drawing changed. The work is streamed off the main actor, so this is not proof of a main-thread freeze, but the UI deliberately waits for it.
Proposed experience
Skip audio preparation for drawing-only history changes. Coalesce rapid volume adjustments and update a live mix where it can match exported sound. Cache a prepared mix by the relevant audio/timeline revision, invalidate only what changed, and preserve failure handling that prevents stale muted playback. Show small in-context updating feedback rather than repeatedly blocking the entire audio form.
User benefit
Volume balancing and undo become fluid, especially on longer reviews, and users can hear the result of an adjustment promptly.
Priority rationale
Potentially large benefit across frequent actions, discounted for media complexity and the need to measure the actual bottleneck before selecting an implementation.
How to evaluate it
Measure time from gain/mute/undo action to usable playback on short and long reviews; verify preview/export audio agreement and failure recovery.

#6

Accessibility

Make every core workflow usable with larger text and assistive controls

Priority 6 of 32Difficult effortLarger improvement

Existing support and remaining gaps confirmed in code; full assistive-use audit remains open

Current experience
Many controls already have labels and 44-point targets, the timing handles have VoiceOver adjustment actions, and numeric movement controls exist. However, custom drawing creation still depends on canvas gestures; pinch/two-finger pan have no equivalent visible zoom/pan controls. Several fixed horizontal rows and 150-point drawing cards risk poor reflow. Screenshot tests exist but do not establish end-to-end VoiceOver usability.
Proposed experience
Reflow dense rows vertically at accessibility sizes, keep actions reachable, and expose stateful names and values for custom controls. Provide Add drawing using a centered default shape, followed by accessible position/size controls; retain text-entry alternatives. Add explicit zoom controls and an accessible drawing/take list with context actions. Use labels, shapes, and selection marks alongside color, and announce meaningful state changes without reading every meter tick.
User benefit
Improves accuracy and readability for everyone while making the app usable for people who cannot precisely drag, pinch, or read small text.
Priority rationale
Cross-cutting product quality with broad impact. The whole workflow is difficult; obvious row reflow and label corrections can be delivered early.
How to evaluate it
Complete import, creation, timing, narration, undo, and export with VoiceOver and large text on a small iPhone; include Voice Control and reduced motion checks.

#7

Timeline & playback

Explain original-video time and finished-review time consistently

Priority 7 of 32Moderate effortQuick-start package

Code confirmed; the recording demonstrates the two different durations

Current experience
The main slider and visible timestamp use outputTime, which includes narration pauses. Thumbnails, drawing timing, loop boundaries, and saved position use source-video time. Take display and export use the review/output timeline. The position slider's VoiceOver value currently reports source time even though its value is output time.
Proposed experience
Use finished-review time for the primary timeline and show inserted pauses as clearly labeled spans. Priority rationale
High conceptual value and a dependency for the larger timeline, loop, and narration redesigns. Some wording and accessibility fixes are easy.
How to evaluate it
Use a 10-second source with a 5-second hold: users should understand the 15-second review and select the same moment from every relevant control.

#9

Import & responsiveness

Turn import into a clear, recoverable sequence

Priority 9 of 32Moderate effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed; present-day import latency is not measured here

Current experience
Import already distinguishes getting the video from Photos, copying, preparing, and checking; it has cancellation, a 15-second waiting explanation, finite background handling, and compatible-copy recovery for specific failures. The current signed configuration uses compatible Photos delivery with provider-progress sampling disabled. The home is disabled under a small progress overlay, and the editor subsequently prepares audio.
Proposed experience
Present one continuous preparation card from selection through editor readiness: Getting video, Copying, Preparing preview, Ready. Distinguish the current stage from overall completion; use progress only when measured, and retain elapsed time when duration is unknown. Keep Cancel stable. Offer error-specific actions such as Choose again, Choose from Files, or Choose compatible copy. If a fresh selection is required, say so. Preserve the existing distinction between possible iCloud work and confirmed downloading.
User benefit
Users know what is happening, whether they can leave the app, and how to recover without repeatedly guessing or importing duplicates.
Priority rationale
High trust value, but the app already has much of the underlying behavior. UX refinements must accompany, not substitute for, measured import-performance work.
How to evaluate it
Observe local and cloud-dependent imports, cancellation, background expiration, and failure/reselection; test comprehension without promising unlimited background execution.

#10

Narration & audio

Support a natural freeze, draw, and explain workflow

Priority 10 of 32Difficult effortLarger improvement

Current restriction is code confirmed; proposed interaction needs prototyping

Current experience
Recording allows Pause video/Resume video while the microphone continues. The video canvas and drawing mode are disabled throughout recording. A take begins only after the player advances, so users cannot simply start speaking on an already frozen frame.
Proposed experience
Let users choose 'Narrate from this frame' and start with the video frozen. While it is frozen, allow a small drawing palette or temporary spotlight pointer, then resume playback without stopping the microphone. Distinguish drawings shown throughout the held frame from gestures replayed in speech order; begin with the simpler explicitly chosen behavior. Until this is implemented, a low-risk interim option is to mark a moment during narration for drawing afterward.
User benefit
Matches the way a coach explains a technique: stop, point at the detail, explain it, and continue. It could become a defining Fresh Frame interaction.
Priority rationale
High creative upside, but recording transactions, drawing timing inside holds, recovery, and export semantics make this a deliberate later milestone rather than a simple unlock.
How to evaluate it
Prototype with coaches before changing media semantics; verify record/pause/draw/undo/stop/reopen and identical timing in the exported MP4.

#11

Narration & audio

Make narration pauses understandable and safe to edit

Priority 11 of 32Moderate effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed

Current experience
Pauses live in a separate Narration pauses section. Deleting or muting a take keeps its video pauses. Remove pause immediately removes that hold and all audio placements attached to it; the section explains this in small text. The relationship between a take and its pauses is not visually connected.
Proposed experience
Show each frozen span beside the takes that use it, with duration and a frame thumbnail. Before removing a pause, identify the affected takes and explain that audio within that interval will be removed. Offer Cancel and an explicit 'Remove pause and its audio' action, followed by local Undo. When deleting a take, explain which pauses remain and link to them; keep pauses by default. Do not imply that a pause belongs exclusively to one take.
User benefit
Avoids surprising silent freezes and accidental removal of commentary, while making the existing timeline model comprehensible.
Priority rationale
A targeted trust improvement around a powerful but non-obvious feature, with less scope than editing the entire timeline model.
How to evaluate it
Test two takes sharing a pause, delete one, then remove the pause and undo; users should predict the result before confirming.

#12

Export & sharing

Make export a visual choice with sensible presets

Priority 12 of 32Moderate effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed; completion preview visually observed

Current experience
The export form offers a full/selected-range toggle, numeric start/end fields, Use loop range, longest-edge pixel choices, and Smaller file/Standard/High quality. How to evaluate it
Ask users to export one narrated moment for messaging and the full review at high quality; verify range boundaries and actual output match the preview.

#13

Save & recovery

Show save state and put Undo beside consequential actions

Priority 13 of 32Easy effortQuick win

Code confirmed

Current experience
Canvas edits and audio changes save immediately; the drawing inspector has a separate staged Save/Cancel contract. The main editor has Undo/Redo icons but no persistent saved-state label. Its history holds up to 50 document snapshots in memory and resets when a new editor session is created. Audio screens tell users to return to the editor for Undo.
Proposed experience
Show a quiet 'Saved on this iPhone' state after successful commits and an explicit 'Unsaved drawing changes' state for inspector drafts. After deleting a drawing or take, removing a pause, or changing a mix, provide a brief local Undo action with an accessible alternative that does not depend on a fast tap. Describe the operation in undo feedback. Keep existing commit semantics; never claim that an unsaved draft or failed write is saved.
User benefit
Reduces anxiety about losing work and makes recovery available at the moment users notice a mistake.
Priority rationale
Low effort and broad confidence benefit; it should accompany other UX changes rather than wait for a full redesign.
How to evaluate it
Verify every displayed state against actual persistence, reopen behavior, failed saves, and the scope of the available Undo operation.

#14

Timeline & playback

Make looping a visible range rather than a menu sequence

Priority 14 of 32Moderate effortLarger improvement

Code confirmed

Current experience
Loop is a menu containing Start here, End here, on/off, and a numeric range. It exists only outside Draw and recording. Selecting an invalid boundary produces an error. The native session already persists loop settings.
Proposed experience
Expose an A–B range directly on the timeline with two accessible handles, a Loop toggle, and a small range label. Let users set a boundary at the playhead, drag either edge, or choose 'Loop this drawing/take.' Clamp or explain invalid ranges inline. How to choose and validate the next work

Before building the major redesign, use a small prototype and real review tasks with both first-time users and people who regularly annotate or narrate video. Keep a baseline of the current app; compare task completion, navigation steps, mistakes, and user confidence. The following are proposed evaluation tasks, not tests already performed.

TaskScenarioUseful evidence
Find the momentFirst-time and experienced users locate a specific action in a short clip and a longer review.Time to correct position, overshoots, confusion about the active clock.
Edit a drawingCreate an arrow, move it, adjust its duration, replay, and undo.Extra navigation, mode mistakes, ability to see the video and understand Save/Cancel.
Explain a frozen frameRecord, freeze, keep speaking, resume, stop, and replay.Whether recording state and pause ownership are correctly understood; usable controls at large text sizes.
Correct narrationIdentify a take, change its level, align it, delete/recover it.Video visibility, action-to-playback delay, and avoidable rebuilds.
Share the right resultExport one narrated range and then reshare it after reopening.Range accuracy, comprehension of output options, unnecessary rerendering.
Recover and accessUse denied-permission recovery, storage tools, backups, and VoiceOver workflows.Task completion, preserved media, clear destructive scope, non-gesture alternatives.

For media-related changes, preserve original assets, source-time annotation meaning, held-frame timing, preview/export agreement, atomic saves, backup compatibility, and existing reviews. Measure local and cloud-dependent import cases separately. A progress redesign does not establish a speed improvement, and simulator results do not establish microphone or physical-touch behavior.

After the review decisions, the most useful development brief would select a small group of related ranks, define before/after interactions, and state observable acceptance criteria. No development was started as part of this assessment.
